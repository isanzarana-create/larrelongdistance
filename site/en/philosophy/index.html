What is
Larre

Etymology, manifesto and the list of things we will never do.

Etymology

larre [ ˈla.re ] · Basque · noun

In its most literal sense, larre names the upland meadow and pasture: open, uncultivated land where livestock graze freely. It also means open mountain and ground without marked paths, by extension any wild and rugged terrain, and, figuratively, what has not been tamed: what still belongs to everyone and to no one.

In a single syllable, the word holds the whole map of Álava: the beech forests of Izki Natural Park and the oak woods of Valderejo Natural Park; the terraced vineyards of the Rioja Alavesa below the Toloño range; the limestone ground and gorges of Okina; the cereal plain of the Llanada Alavesa; the high pastoral paths of the Entzia range; and the vast Ullíbarri-Gamboa reservoir, the territory's inner mirror.

The chosen noun evokes Álava's pastoral culture: transhumant flocks that for centuries stitched the four Álavas together in one seasonal movement, crossing forest, vineyard, mountain and plain. Larre is that same crossing, now on foot.

Manifesto

We leave Vitoria-Gasteiz heading south and reach Peñacerrada-Urizaharra and the vineyards of Laguardia, beneath the Toloño range, facing the Ebro valley and the first lands of Rioja.

We cross the villages of the Rioja Alavesa, climb towards Santa Cruz de Campezo and up into the Entzia range, where the flocks of Álava's last transhumant shepherds still graze, before returning through Agurain to Vitoria-Gasteiz.

226.9 km that stitch the four Álavas into a single line.

Forest, vineyard, mountain and plain. Without assistance, without classic aid stations, without compulsory rest. With a GPS track, a brevet card and the certainty that the mountain is earned step by step.

  • Total self-sufficiency
  • No classic aid stations
  • Zero waste in the mountains
  • GPS tracker required
  • Merged roadbook-brevet
  • Mandatory gear checked
  • Authored audiovisual production
  • Small, demanding community
  • Durable materials · no event kit
  • Absolute respect for the territory

Is it a race?

No.

Although some will treat it as one, Larre is a personal challenge. For many, the challenge will simply be to finish.

There will be a final ranking by order of arrival. But, following the traditions of ultra-cycling, bikepacking and long self-supported routes, there will be no prize for the fastest participant except the honour of arriving first.

No trophies are handed out here for speed. What is handed out is the privilege of having been there.

The culture of self-sufficiency understands that victory is not in beating the person next to you, but in holding your pace for 226.9 kilometres of mountain without anyone solving your water, your dinner or your sleep. That is the only competition that matters.

Zero Code

The only trace we leave in the mountains is our footprints.

No wrappers, no stickers, no gels, no strips of tape. What goes into the mountains with the runner leaves the mountains with the runner.

It is not a slogan. It is a condition of entry. Every brevet card carries a zero-waste protocol signed at the briefing. Dropping a single piece of litter on the course, documented by photo, organiser, volunteer, another runner or scouting camera, means immediate disqualification and a ban from future editions. No nuances, no prior warning, no second chance.

That severity is deliberate. It is the only way to build a community that understands the privilege of being able to cross Toloño and Entzia. Most runners who already go self-supported live it that way naturally; we write it down so that nobody arrives without knowing.

The second axis is the durability of things. We produce few objects, make them to last for years, and design each one thinking of how it will be repaired before how it will be thrown away. The 1st Edition cap should still be in use in 2032. The brevet card is not paper: it is FSC card meant to be framed. The bib is technical fabric with reinforced stitching, not a piece of Tyvek that lasts a morning.

The most sustainable thing we produce is what we do not produce. Every year we review the whole pack and remove whatever adds nothing. We do not give things away for the sake of it. If a T-shirt is not the best T-shirt you will own this year, we do not give it to you.

We say NO long before we say YES

Almost everything that defines Larre is a list of things we will not do. Each NO on this page is a conscious decision: something the inertia of the trail-running market would push us to do and that we have ruled out as inconsistent with the project.

Saying NO is the only way to protect what matters. This list is neither exhaustive nor final: it grows every year as we learn what erodes the event and what sustains it.

  • / 01

    No hot aid stations in the mountains

    Self-sufficiency does not include a hot stew halfway round. Either you cook it yourself, or you buy it at a bar open to the public.

  • / 02

    No inflatable start arch

    PVC, generator, transport, waste. The start is where the runners are, not where the sponsored inflatable is.

  • / 03

    No music or announcer at the start

    This is not a village fair. The mountains do not need loudspeakers. The silence before the first step is part of the rite.

  • / 04

    No plastic medals or heat-sealed screen printing

    The finisher medal is solid recycled metal, die-cut by a supplier in the Basque Country. It is heavy, it lasts, it is handed down. It is not a badge.

  • / 05

    No cash prize for the winner

    There is no bonus for speed. Every finisher receives the same gift. The privilege of the first is being first, nothing more.

  • / 06

    No polyester “free” T-shirt

    Microplastic in the wash, thrown away in two years. The Larre shirt is certified organic cotton, a single design each edition. If you would not buy it, we do not give it to you.

  • / 07

    No more places if “people are left out”

    300 is 300. The 2nd Edition will be 300. The 10th Edition will be 300. The number is identity, not a market parameter. If you do not get in, you come back next year.

  • / 08

    No barriers, carpet or red carpet in villages

    We pass through villages that live all year round. A runner's passing should be visible but discreet. No ephemeral infrastructure in squares and streets.

This list will grow every year. If a decision we take today turns out to be inconsistent with the project, next year it joins the NO list and stays there. It is the only honest way to improve: documenting what we rule out, not just what we defend.

Territory · zero impact, honestly

Larre's sustainability is not a badge on a website: it is a series of technical decisions at every stage of the project. No greenwashing and no green marketing: real, measured reduction and symbolic offsetting of the unavoidable through Retree the Planet.

  • A

    Pack cut to 4 essentials

    Cap and organic cotton T-shirt (single design per year · collectable), roadbook-brevet merged into FSC card, finisher medal in recycled metal. Technical bib separately as an operational item. No single-use plastics, no decorative merchandise.

  • B

    Local production

    Pack made in Álava where possible: local screen printing, sewing in workshops of the territory, and wines and preserves for the finish from producers within 30 km of the route.

  • C

    Reusable trackers

    GPS devices rented year after year, not consumables. Cases and straps reused. All the event's electronics are managed through rental, not purchase.

  • D

    Clean mobility

    Insistent communication to encourage train over car. Shared and, where possible, electric staff vehicles.

  • E

    Carbon footprint

    Annual calculation of the event's emissions (transport, production, staff accommodation) and offsetting through reforestation with Retree the Planet and local planting with HAZI / Provincial Council projects.

  • F

    Durability above all

    Every item in the pack is designed to last years, not hours. The cap should still be in use in 2032. The roadbook-brevet should be framed, not thrown away. The organic cotton T-shirt, a single design each edition, becomes a collector's item: whoever returns to Larre builds up a different piece every year. It is the antithesis of the event kit.
